Which AI model should I use?

JobHunt asks you to bring your own model, which is a real question with a real cost attached. We tested some so you don't have to. If you want to be told what to pick, read the next paragraph and stop.

Recommended
Ministral 14B, via OpenRouter

It gives the same answer twice, passes both of our accuracy suites over repeated runs, and costs about 12¢ per 100 job postings. Flat $0.20 per million tokens in either direction, so the bill doesn't move around either.

Changed in August 2026. We previously recommended DeepSeek V4 Flash on a cheaper headline price. Then we measured consistency and found it returns different verdicts for identical input — enough to move a score 10–16 points on a re-run. It has also announced price rises.

Setting it up

  1. Create an account at openrouter.ai and add a few dollars of credit.
  2. Create an API key and copy it.
  3. In JobHunt: Settings → AI, set Provider to OpenRouter, paste the key, click Fetch Models, and choose mistralai/ministral-14b-2512.
  4. JobHunt will ask you to confirm before it sends anything to a cloud provider. That prompt is deliberate — see below.

You can change this later at any time, and changing it doesn't rescore the jobs you already have unless you ask it to. It's a low-stakes decision.

If you'd rather pay nothing

Run a model on your own Mac with LM Studio or Ollama, and point JobHunt at http://127.0.0.1:1234. It costs nothing, and no job description or résumé ever leaves your machine.

The honest trade-off: local models are slower, and on consumer hardware they are generally less reliable at the structured extraction JobHunt asks for — a malformed response means a job fails to extract and gets retried. We have not benchmarked local models, so we can't tell you how much accuracy you give up. If privacy is the priority, this is the option; if accuracy is, it isn't.

Consistency: does it give the same answer twice?

This turned out to matter more than the accuracy score, and we didn't measure it at first. We sent the byte-identical scoring request to each model several times, at temperature 0, and counted how often the verdict on a requirement changed between runs.

ModelVerdicts that changedScore swing
Claude Haiku 4.50 of 150 points

Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite5 of 15~10 points

Re-measured on 2026-08-09, and the swings are larger than the table above. The numbers above came from one posting with 15 requirements. A second, independent measurement — a different posting with 7 requirements, scored repeatedly through the app's own scoring path — found considerably more movement:

ModelUsable runsScoresSpreadVerdicts changed
Claude Haiku 4.5473, 69, 65, 728 points1 of 7
Ministral 14B567, 67, 62, 81, 7219 points1 of 7

Two things follow. The ordering holds — Haiku is still the steadier of the two, by roughly a factor of two. But neither model is near-deterministic, and the "0 points" above should be read as "0 on that posting", not as a property of the model. A job's score can move by 8 to 19 points between identical requests, which is more than enough to reorder your list around a threshold.

Caveat, stated plainly: these are 4–5 usable samples per model on a single posting. That is enough to show the earlier figures were optimistic — one 19-point swing disproves "~2 points" — but not enough to rank models precisely. We are reporting it because publishing a consistency claim we could no longer reproduce would be worse than publishing an uncertain one.

Separately, and affecting both models: 2 to 4 of every 6–8 requests returned an empty response and had to be discarded. That is an aggregator-level failure rather than a model one; the app retries, so it shows up as slowness rather than as an error.

Temperature 0 does not make hosted inference deterministic. Batched serving means the result can depend on what else was in the batch, and aggregators may route the same request to a different host each time — we saw one model served by five different providers across eight calls. Pinning the provider reduced this but did not remove it.

An unstable model still ranks roughly correctly, but a job near your minimum-score threshold can move across it on a re-score. If that matters to you, prefer a model higher up this table.

OpenRouter or DeepSeek directly?

The same model is available two ways. OpenRouter is one account and one key that reaches many models, so switching later is a dropdown rather than a signup — that's why we recommend it. DeepSeek's own API is typically cheaper for the same model and involves one fewer company handling your data.

Going direct works today via Provider → Custom with DeepSeek's base URL, but you'll have to type the model name yourself and JobHunt won't be able to estimate your costs. Making DeepSeek a first-class provider is on our list.

What gets sent

With a cloud provider, JobHunt sends the job description text for extraction, and the description plus your résumé text for fit scoring. That's your employment history going to a third party, which is why JobHunt asks for explicit consent the first time and won't send anything before you agree. With a local model, none of this leaves your Mac. See the Privacy Policy.
